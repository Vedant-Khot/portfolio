Chapter 1: Strategic Foundations & Domain Physics

Project Overview

The project titled “WiDS Datathon 2026” focuses on predicting whether an active wildfire will reach within 5 km of an evacuation zone within 72 hours using advanced Machine Learning and Survival Analysis techniques. The project was developed under the guidance of Prof. Sneha Varur in the Department of Computer Science and Engineering (Artificial Intelligence).

This work is interdisciplinary and combines survival analysis, ensemble learning, graph neural networks (GNNs), spatial-temporal data analysis, disaster prediction, and climate & social impact analytics. The project is highly aligned with current trends in AI for Climate, Disaster Intelligence, Geo-spatial AI, Risk Forecasting, and Smart Emergency Systems.

1. Core Problem Understanding

The system predicts: “Will a wildfire reach within 5 km of an evacuation zone in the next 72 hours?”

This is not a simple classification problem. It is a time-to-event prediction problem, a survival analysis problem, and a spatio-temporal forecasting problem. The project attempts to understand fire growth, fire movement direction, speed, distance reduction, infrastructure threat, and temporal progression. Rather than evaluating the fire in isolation, it focuses on the active dynamics of distance contraction between the hazard boundary and civil assets.

2. Why This Problem is Important

Wildfires globally are increasing because of climate change, drought, heat waves, deforestation, and human activity. The impacts of uncontrolled wildfire breaches are devastating, leading to massive loss of human life, infrastructure destruction, acute air pollution, ecological collapse, and huge economic losses.

The project has real-world operational importance because it can support emergency evacuation systems, fire department planning, infrastructure protection, government disaster response, and smart city resilience systems. By shifting early warnings from binary alerts to specific temporal risk horizons, rescue teams can deploy supplies, prioritize vulnerable groups, and map optimal transit paths.

5. Strong Domain Understanding

One of the strongest parts of this project is the deep domain understanding. The team did not simply apply machine learning blindly to tabular fields. They thoroughly understood fire spread physics, spatial behavior, distance progression, survival prediction logic, and disaster management implications. This is extremely important in real AI projects, where physical constraints dictate the mathematical boundaries of models.

Spatio-Temporal Domain Constraint

Unlike traditional ML problems that treat observations as independent, wildfire risk modeling requires tracking spatial coordinates and temporal trends together. A fire's movement depends heavily on its previous movement speed and spread direction, which makes spatial kinematics critical for forecasting risk.

Chapter 3: Survival Analysis & Censoring Mechanics

6. Survival Analysis — Advanced Concept

The project uses Survival Analysis, a highly advanced mathematical framework usually reserved for healthcare (clinical trials), reliability engineering (mechanical failures), and risk modeling. In this project, it is adapted for wildfire prediction.

Instead of predicting simple binary outcomes like "Fire / No Fire," the survival model answers a much more useful question: “How long until the wildfire becomes dangerous and breaches the 5 km boundary?” This allows emergency planners to understand the hazard's progression over time, making the system far more realistic for real-world decision-making.

7. Censoring Handling

A highly advanced concept included in the project is the proper mathematical handling of Censoring.

In risk forecasting, some wildfires never reach the evacuation zone within the 72-hour observation window. Standard classification models might treat these as simple "zeros" (failures). However, survival analysis correctly identifies them as “censored observations”—meaning the event of interest did not occur during the observation period, but could occur later.

This prevents the model from treating safe, long-duration fires as simple negative cases, ensuring unbiased risk estimations. Integrating censoring mechanics demonstrates excellent technical and mathematical maturity.

Survival vs Classification Comparison
  • Binary Classification: Outputs a flat probability. Misses *when* the breach occurs. Treats a fire breaching at 2 hours the same as one breaching at 70 hours.
  • Survival Analysis: Generates a full continuous risk curve over the entire 72-hour window. Captures both the risk likelihood and the speed of threat progression.

Chapter 4: Preprocessing, EDA & Feature Selection

9. Data Preprocessing Analysis

The preprocessing pipeline is properly structured to address data quality and volume challenges:

A. Data Generation / Expansion: Because the dataset had only 220 distinct records, temporal expansion was used. Generating multiple temporal slices from the same event reduces overfitting, increases training diversity, and improves the model's ability to generalize to new, unseen fire patterns.

B. Data Cleaning: Standard but essential data cleaning steps were performed, including handling missing values, duplicate removal, error correction, and consistency checking to ensure data integrity.

C. Feature Scaling: Robust feature scaling was applied. This ensures numerical stability, faster model convergence, and balanced contributions from distance metrics, growth metrics, and speed-based variables.

10. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

The EDA section provides critical insights into the structure and patterns within the data:

A. Class Imbalance: The dataset exhibits moderate imbalance (68.8% Event=0, 31.2% Event=1). SMOTE oversampling and class weighting inside survival loss functions ensure balanced model performance.

B. Seasonal Trend Analysis: 62% of all observed wildfire breaches occur during the July–August window. This temporal seasonality is crucial for disaster forecasting and anticipating seasonal resource demands.

C. Correlation Analysis: Top predictors include Time-to-hit, Distance to infrastructure, Closing speed, and Growth rate — validating the project's physical domain assumptions.

14. Why Ensemble Learning Was Smart

Combining these diverse architectures improves model performance and reliability. **XGBoost Cox** provides excellent risk ranking, the **AFT model** estimates the actual time to breach, the **Random Survival Forest** captures complex non-linear feature interactions, and the **Weibull model** provides smooth statistical baselines. Combining these individual strengths yields a highly robust, accurate, and stable predictive system.

15. Performance Evaluation

The project achieved an outstanding **0.981 C-Index** with the Consolidated Blend Ensemble. A Concordance Index near 0.98 indicates exceptionally strong predictive accuracy and excellent risk-ranking capabilities, meaning the model can highly reliably distinguish between fast-moving threats and slower, safer fires.

16. Optimization Techniques

The high predictive performance is driven by sophisticated optimization techniques:

A. Rank-Based Blending: A competition-level technique that blends hazard rankings rather than raw probability values, significantly improving calibration and model stability.

B. Confidence Weighting: Dynamically weights training samples, assigning higher importance to high-resolution observations to reduce the impact of noisy alerts.

C. Survival Constraints: Enforces physical constraints, including monotonicity (risk must never decrease over time) and forecast horizon consistency, ensuring realistic predictions.

17. Training Curves Analysis

Training diagnostic curves demonstrate stable convergence without severe overfitting. The smooth loss decay and consistent validation scores confirm excellent hyperparameter tuning, effective preprocessing, and a highly stable learning pipeline.
