Key Research & Technical Learnings

Predicting active wildfire evacuation zone breaches involves unique mathematical, spatial, and statistical challenges. Below is a comprehensive catalog of the core topics involved in the project, documenting the theoretical concepts, structural gotchas, and research-level methodologies developed to solve them.

1. Survival Analysis & Right-Censoring Mechanics

Standard classification frameworks treat predictions as flat binary tasks, discarding the critical temporal dimension of risk. In contrast, this project correctly frames threat modeling as a **Time-to-Event (Survival) prediction problem**.

Technical Insight — The Censoring Deficit

Naive vs. Kaplan-Meier estimates: The training dataset has a naive hit rate of 31.2% by 72 hours. However, the true Kaplan-Meier population estimate stands at ~55%. This massive gap occurs because many fires located further away are right-censored—they never breach within the 72-hour window, but are highly likely to breach if observed longer. Modeling the censoring mechanism ensures risk projections remain unbiased.

2. Structural Zero-Inflation in Spatial Tracking

The dataset suffers from extreme zero-inflation across kinematics and growth features (88–92% zero values). Naive imputation breaks survival modeling. The team diagnosed this as a **structural sensor limitation** rather than random missingness.

Subpopulation Segmentation

72.4% of fires have only a single perimeter observation in the first 5 hours. Growth, speed, and heading are computed as differences between perimeters; without a second snapshot, these features default to zero. The team resolved this by segmenting the data into a Single-Perimeter cohort (modeled strictly on static initial proximity) and a Multi-Perimeter cohort (modeled with full kinematic tracking).

3. Domain Physics & The Proximity "Oracle"

Machine learning models are highly prone to overfitting on statistical noise if physical boundaries are ignored. Applying domain constraints to features proved to be the most powerful modeling innovation.

  • The 4,674m Threshold: Spatial analysis revealed that 100% of fires starting under 4,674 meters breached the evacuation zone boundary, whereas 0% of fires starting at or above 5,000 meters breached. This clean physical separation bounds model risk.
  • Logarithmic Linearization: Distance features scale exponentially. Applying log-compression, log_dist_min = log1p(dist_min_ci_0_5h), expanded feature variance and boosted Pearson correlation from −0.481 to a highly linear −0.812, providing robust gradient splits.
4. Sensor Placeholder Bias (Wind Bearing Trap)

Raw datasets frequently contain hidden placeholders that leak bias into tree split algorithms. A major finding was the **0° North wind bearing default**.

Wind Heading Placeholder

For 160 of the 221 training fires, the wind spread direction defaulted to `spread_bearing_cos = 1.0` (pointing exactly 0° North). Tree-based models naively learn that fires headed North are highly dangerous, corrupting predictions. The team resolved this by introducing a binary mask feature bearing_is_real, instructing the ensemble to ignore the bearing signal for placeholder records.

5. Probability Calibration & Metrics Rigor

The datathon evaluation metric heavily weights Brier Scores, making probability calibration far more important than raw classification accuracy. The team discovered that standard Platt Scaling falls short on right-censored training horizons.

  • Isotonic Calibration: Replaced parametric probability scaling with per-fold isotonic regression to map raw model scores back to actual empirical breach rates, preventing extreme probability collapse at the 72h window ceiling.
  • Heuristic Pitfalls: Early model attempts to use exponential decay probability models (e.g. 1 - exp(-k * risk * t)) yielded poor scores because they failed to capture non-parametric temporal hazard rates. Isotonic calibration remains mathematically mandatory.
6. Digital Twin Augmentation & CV Stabilization

Small datasets (only 221 training fires) limit model depth and make validation folds unstable. The team bypassed this limitation through **Data Augmentation and Digital Twins**.

By applying randomized, independent-jitter coordinates to historical fire boundaries, the team generated 1,200 synthetic digital twin fires. This expanded training diversity and stabilized cross-validation folds, reducing Out-of-Fold variance and protecting the final ensemble from public leaderboard overfitting.
